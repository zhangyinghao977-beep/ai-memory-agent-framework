# Historical experiment summaries

Review cutoff: 2026-11-06. This page summarizes existing T1–T10 reports. **It is neither the original frozen evidence package nor an independent reproduction.** The review read existing reports and recorded their SHA256 hashes; it did not rerun training or evaluation. Original weights, databases, item-level outputs, and receipts are not distributed in this preview.

These results come from different controlled synthetic tasks, model versions, and budgets. They cannot be combined into a claim that a complete general AI has been implemented. T1–T10 do not depend on the 119M pretrained backbone or external natural-language corpora. “Completed” below means the number of historical stages/tasks reported as finished, **not a test pass rate or a fraction of theoretical claims established**.

## Recorded progress and counterexamples

| Experiment | Reported completion | Observation retained | Limitation or failure retained |
|---|---:|---|---|
| T1: layered spiral training and composition | First run 63/63; second run 30/30 | Structured models use supplied structure in a generated composition task. For the second run's XL “new combination × conflict” metric, A-spiral scores are 94.9%/95.4%/95.0% across three seeds. | The linear-prior D′ scores are 96.4%/96.3%/96.4%, so this is not a win over every baseline. Identity constraints include `identity_bias=6`; a pre-generated stream simulates arrival; methods were not individually tuned. It does not establish general superiority over Transformers. |
| T2: layered repair after changes | 72/72 | Controlled C1/C2/C3 changes route to KA_local, EA, and CA repair. Recorded update counts are approximately 2.16%/5.51%/2.80% of retraining. | The three changes were deliberately constructed to affect one layer each. This does not establish autonomous diagnosis of arbitrary changes. Update ratios are not complete compute, time, or storage costs. |
| T3: mutual support and stability | 12/12 | Reported bidirectional scores are 95.6%–96.8%, versus 85.9%–86.1% for one-way processing, supporting a limited comparison in this task. | Indirect packages in the one-way setup were never trained; part of the difference follows from construction. Stopping conditions were simulated afterward on existing trajectories, not independently validated online. |
| T4: persistence, repair, and recovery scaffolding | 22/22 | The report records 96.9%–97.3% on the old world, 93.6%–95.8% on new packages, and 96.4%–97.1% on a new genre; six injected-crash recovery contracts passed. | “Ten days” is synthetic time with 1,024 questions per day, not ten days of actual continuous operation. The fixed FSM is research scaffolding and does not write code; forgetting is not implemented. These are not results for the Hongchenxian work-event chain or its runtime capabilities. |
| T5: content selection, lattice and attention | 90/90 | Without the lattice, attention is near chance within the tested budget: 52.0%/50.3%/50.4%. Lattice-assisted mutual-support results can be high but vary substantially by seed. | P2 “only attention learns,” P3 “transfer to new packages,” and P5 “mean communication suffices for anchor questions” fail their original criteria. Mutual-support attention scores are 98.8%/68.6%/95.4%; new-package scores are 91.0%/60.2%/84.6%. This cannot prove attention necessary; handwritten rules already reach 100%. |
| T6: lattice components and alternative comparators | 70/70 | Pairwise comparison without softmax reaches 99.2%–99.8% on the specified mutual-support task; the supplied lattice comparator reaches 100% in the listed old/new-package cases. | P1 “reference vectors alone suffice” fails: mutual-support scores are 51.4%–61.3%. The `sim` structure is handwritten for the task. Neither attention's necessity nor a generally superior algorithm follows. |
| T7: noisy reference vectors | 90/90 | Lattice comparison tracks vector quality, at most 0.8 percentage points from the cosine baseline; it is not worse than attention in the tested new-package cases. | N3 “learning compensates for ambiguity” fails: pairwise comparison is roughly 9.8–11.6 points below the baseline on the high-noise mutual-support task. Only fixed per-object offset noise was tested; vectors are still supplied by humans. |
| T8: forming reference vectors from event chains | 110/110 | The co-occurrence-plus-SVD implementation forms vectors without package IDs or answer labels. Lattice comparison reaches 100% on new-package anchor questions and 96.4% on mutual support. Shuffling chains reduces results toward chance, supporting the role of this controlled chain signal. | M4 “learned comparators also use these vectors reliably” fails. New-package mutual-support scores are 64.5%–79.4% for pairwise comparison and 55.1%–71.5% for attention. Only shared structure is formed, not differences; relation rules remain supplied. Old/new coordinates are separate and mixed chains untested. Same-package co-occurrence is a strong artificial generation signal. |
| T9: forming and replacing relation tables | 65/65 | With full experience, the 12-parameter lattice comparator retains 100% on new-package anchor questions and 96.4% on mutual support. Corrupting relation tables sharply reduces performance. A transition can be observed when replacing the table after a controlled world change. | R4–R8 fail their original criteria; partial curve improvements must not relabel them as passed. Full-rule mutual-support attention is about 49.9%–50.4%, and 49.6%–51.7% on new packages. Rules are visible-query/relation correspondences. The system does not detect changes; a window replaces old information without preserving old versions. Comparator structure, formation formula, and window are human-designed. |
| T10: initial relation-embedding scale | 50/50 | Initial scales of 0.25 and 0 raise mean mutual-support pairwise scores from T9's 74.8% to 96.7%/98.4%. The zero-initialized variant reaches 95.9% after controlled table replacement, versus 96.9% under the original-world window condition. | **S2 fails:** zero-initialized attention averages only 54.4% on existing packages and 51.5% on new ones. **S7 confirms a limitation:** the zero-initialized pairwise comparator still averages 69.1% on new packages, so transfer remains unresolved. The adjustment was motivated by T9 results and is a task-specific repair; only a few initial scales were tried and the full mechanism is unexplained. |

The main T5–T10 metric is accuracy on questions with two candidates. Chance is referenced as 50%, but selecting another position counts as incorrect, so scores can be lower. Seed ranges and means are not overall accuracy across all tasks. “Pass/fail” refers to the original reports' local prediction criteria, not the entire architecture.

T1–T7 explicitly use human-supplied packages, reference frames, or structural signals. T8/T9 reduce some directly supplied content, but retain artificial generation mechanisms, representation forms, comparator structures, and query formats. Counterexamples and distinctions between properties guaranteed by construction and empirical findings must remain visible. T5–T10 explicitly do not measure potential Φ or event-chain entropy. This preview claims no proof of consciousness, fully autonomous knowledge formation, general transfer, or complete AI.
