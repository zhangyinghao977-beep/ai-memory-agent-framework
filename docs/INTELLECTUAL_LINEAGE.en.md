# Intellectual lineage and tribute: bringing the ideas together as “glue”

[简体中文](INTELLECTUAL_LINEAGE.md) · [Repository](../README.en.md) · [Correspondences and evaluation boundaries](../projects/the-abyss/EVALUATION_CORRECTION.en.md)

Recorded: 2026-11-10. This is the project author's interpretation of intellectual sources and a tribute, not a new experimental result or a declaration that complete AI has been achieved.

## How the author understands the contribution

**The author sees their main engineering role as “glue”: assembling ideas from Turing and von Neumann as building blocks according to their own theory.** The author identifies their fundamental judgment as “Logic is the path that makes knowledge reachable,” which they understand as a judgment rooted in logic; the engineering process chiefly draws on the two pioneers’ ideas. In this view, combining logic, memory and the FSM reconnects those ideas under present-day conditions toward the goal of complete AI that continues operating and accumulates experience.

The “puzzle pieces” metaphor expresses the author's overall interpretation. It is more specific than simply being inspired: the project seeks to connect computable processes, learning and experience with the organization of storage, control and execution. The author wants to pursue this combination; we regret that we cannot see where the two pioneers would have taken these questions had their exploration continued.

“Glue” describes the engineering contribution without erasing that core judgment. The judgment is recorded as this project’s proposition, not as a quotation from Turing or von Neumann or an established general theorem of logic. The project still needs concrete representations, interfaces, training and operating mechanisms. It has not established that every engineering choice can be derived, item by item, from the two authors’ original texts.

## What the primary literature supports

- **Turing on rule-governed computation and learning machines.** See the existing [correspondence table](../projects/the-abyss/EVALUATION_CORRECTION.en.md) for computable processes. Section 4 of his 1950 *Computing Machinery and Intelligence* discusses the store, executive unit and control; section 7 discusses a child machine, education and experience. Turing should not be portrayed as considering logic without memory, control or learning. [Retypeset 1950 paper](https://www.csee.umbc.edu/courses/471/papers/turing.pdf)
- **Von Neumann on storage, control and execution working together.** Sections 2.2–2.8 of the 1945 *First Draft of a Report on the EDVAC* distinguish arithmetic, control, memory and input/output; section 14.1 discusses instructions and numbers drawn from the same memory. These are organizational references for the project, not sufficient conditions for complete AI. [Retypeset report](https://people.csail.mit.edu/brooks/idocs/VonNeumann_EDVAC.pdf)
- **Their ideas were not isolated from one another.** On printed page 3 of his 1945 ACE report, Turing explicitly recommends reading it alongside von Neumann's EDVAC report. “Connecting the puzzle pieces” should therefore not become a claim that Turing lacked access to von Neumann's ideas. [ACE report page and transcription](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Alan_Turing_-_Proposed_Electronic_Calculator_(1945).pdf/4)

The linked EDVAC edition is Michael D. Godfrey's corrected retypesetting, not an unedited manuscript scan. This note paraphrases the relevant passages without redistributing the complete historical works or treating the two individuals' contributions as exclusive attribution that erases other participants.

## The meaning and limits of “only a little was missing”

The author's expressions “complete AI as I see it” and “only a little was missing” convey an interpretation of how the ideas fit and a research aspiration. This note does not establish that either pioneer was historically close to implementing complete AI, that each lacked only the other's theory, or that combining them guarantees success.

Historical authority does not replace validation. The project remains a research prototype. Whether mechanisms are implemented, which tasks and resource conditions they work under, and what failures or costs arise must be answered through the relevant code, experiments and original evidence. Existing experimental results, failures and unfinished work remain unchanged.
